Where to take the app, grounded in three inputs: the 10-competitor research, an audit of what ClipZoo's code can actually do today, and fresh research on the TikTok/IG/YouTube posting-API question. The strategy is simple: build the machine that markets Evex, and sell that machine to founders like you.

1 · The strategic call

You said you're a little lost. Here's the compass: you are the customer. You have a real, painful, recurring job — marketing Evex — and no tool serves it well. That's not a limitation; per the competitor research it's exactly the wedge:

  • ReelFarm (closest to this vision) proves the demand for hook+demo videos and slideshows — but it's a TikTok-only "content farm" with browse-only inspiration, weak editing, and no campaign concept.
  • Arcads ($110+/mo) generates great talking heads but is a "generator, not an editor" — users export to CapCut to add hooks, captions, CTAs. In-app editing is a confirmed gap.
  • HeyGen / Higgsfield / CapCut are horizontal giants. Don't compete on avatar realism or editing depth. Borrow their patterns (one-character-many-looks, tap-to-edit text) when needed.

So the positioning line, for now: "ClipZoo — generate your UGC marketing." Not "automate" — automation implies scheduling, accounts, and trust you don't have infrastructure for yet. Generation is the value; automation is the retention feature you add once generation works (P4).

Who it's for (v1)

Indie founders & small teams marketing a B2C app. They have demo footage and an app store listing; they don't have time, editing skills, or a UGC budget. Literally you + Evex.

The promise

Drop in your app demo → get a week of post-ready, proven-format vertical content in under 15 minutes. Editable text everywhere, no CapCut round-trip.

The moat direction

Formats-as-templates + your saved brand/products + (later) performance feedback. Competitors generate clips; ClipZoo learns your app and produces campaigns.

The dogfood metric

Evex ships ≥3 ClipZoo-made posts/week, each produced in <15 min. If a phase doesn't move that metric, cut it.

3 · Your TikTok scheduling question — answered

You asked how hard TikTok API access + scheduling is. Short version: natively, brutal; through your existing Upload-Post integration, nearly free.

Implication for the roadmap: scheduling is not a research problem or an API-access problem. It's a persistence problem (ClipZoo forgets everything after 1 hour). That's why the calendar lands in P4, after P3 gives you a database.

4 · The roadmap

P1 Hook + Demo composer ~1–2 weeksyour clearest need

The ReelFarm-style format you explicitly want: a bold, editable hook card that plays before (or over the first seconds of) your app demo footage, with captions and a CTA end-card. This is one new pipeline function plus one focused screen.

P1 composer: type the hook, pick the style, attach demo footage, generate. The hook text stays editable after generation — the anti-Arcads move.

  • Build: extend hooks.py to render a full-frame 9:16 hook card (reuse its PIL text renderer); new pipeline fn: hook card (2–3s) + demo clip + optional CTA end-card via FFmpeg concat; new POST /api/compose/hook-demo; new "Create" screen in the dashboard.
  • Borrow: Gemini hook suggestions seeded from the app's store listing (reuse saasshorts.py research step) — ReelFarm's Hook Generator, but grounded in your product.
  • Success: a real Evex post produced end-to-end in <15 min, no CapCut.

P2 Format templates: listicle slideshows ~2–3 weeks

Your "best fitness apps → Whoop, Aura, then Evex" idea is a format template: a fill-in-the-blanks structure that renders to a slideshow. Start with exactly two templates — Listicle / Good-Better-Best and Hook+Demo (from P1, saved as a template). Before/after and greenscreen memes wait. This is how you avoid boiling the ocean: templates are data, so adding format #3 later is cheap.

P2: fill in the list, your app is always the payoff slide. Every text box remains editable after render — the wedge no core competitor offers.

  • Build: slide renderer (PIL/Pillow, 1080×1920 — reuse hook-card renderer from P1); template = JSON schema (slots: title, items[], cta); Gemini fills slots from a niche prompt ("best running apps") with your app injected as the winner; export both image set (TikTok photo carousels get outsized reach) and MP4 slideshow (IG/YT).
  • Borrow: ReelFarm's listicle hook patterns; its Pinterest image-sourcing idea can wait — start with solid colored/branded backgrounds + your app screenshots.
  • Success: generate the "best fitness apps" post you described, publish it for Evex, in <10 min.

P3 Content library + first database ~1–2 weeksinfra unlock

Today ClipZoo forgets everything after an hour. Before scheduling can exist, generated content and saved templates need to survive a restart. Smallest possible step: SQLite + SQLAlchemy (single file, zero ops, Docker-volume friendly) with three tables — assets (uploaded demo footage, brand info), creations (generated posts + their editable template JSON), templates (saved formats/hook styles). S3 keeps holding the media bytes; the DB holds metadata and state.

  • "My content" screen: every generated post, its status (draft / published / scheduled), re-open → re-edit text → re-render.
  • Saved brand profile for Evex: name, app store link, screenshots, default CTA — so every template pre-fills.
  • This also unblocks reusable characters later (HeyGen's one-character-many-looks) — a character is just another saved asset.

P4 Calendar + scheduling ~1–2 weekscheap thanks to Upload-Post

Now the automation you wanted — and it's mostly UI, because Upload-Post does the hard part. Persist a scheduled_posts table; on schedule, call the existing /api/social/post with its existing scheduled_date param; render a Buffer-style week/month calendar (the restraint pattern from the Buffer research: two views, drag to reschedule, per-platform color chips).

P4: Buffer-style restraint — a week view, platform-tinted cards, empty-slot affordance. All posting flows through Upload-Post's already-integrated API.
