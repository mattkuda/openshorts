# Post-bridge

> A lightweight, low-cost social scheduler that lets creators upload once and cross-post (or schedule) the same content to up to 9 platforms in ~2 minutes.

## What they do
Post-bridge is a cross-posting and scheduling tool for creators and small teams. You connect your social accounts once, drop in a video or post, and publish (or schedule) it to up to 9 platforms simultaneously from one dashboard — Twitter/X, Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, Bluesky, Threads, and Pinterest. The pitch is time: distribution that used to take 30+ minutes of copy-paste across apps now takes about 2 minutes.

It was built in October 2024 by solo founder Jack Friks, who was manually reposting content while growing his own apps and got tired of incumbent schedulers charging $50–$150/mo for what he saw as a simple task. That origin shapes the whole product: it is deliberately lightweight, fast, and affordable rather than a feature-bloated enterprise suite. It reportedly reached ~$11K MRR as a small indie SaaS, and support is direct from the founder.

Beyond raw scheduling it includes a "Content Studio" with drag-and-drop, brand-customizable viral video templates, so creators can produce and queue content in one continuous flow. Analytics exist on paid plans but are basic and were described as beta-stage — post performance and engagement only, no competitor tracking, custom reports, or white-labeling. There is also a developer API (add-on) for custom integrations.

## Core workflow
1. Sign up (free tier or 7-day trial), connect your social accounts (multiple accounts per platform supported).
2. Upload a video/image or write a post once in the composer.
3. (Optional) Customize the post per platform; optionally build/brand a clip in the Content Studio templates.
4. Pick "post now" or schedule a time; bulk/queue multiple posts at once (incl. carousels and bulk video scheduling).
5. Confirm — content fans out to all selected platforms; review basic analytics afterward on paid plans.

## Key features
- Cross-post to up to 9 platforms from a single composer.
- Scheduling, queues, drafts, and a content calendar.
- Multiple accounts per platform; per-platform post customization.
- Bulk video scheduling and carousel-post support.
- Content Studio: drag-and-drop, brand-customizable viral video templates.
- Basic analytics (paid plans, beta).
- Developer API (add-on) and direct founder support.

## Output / content types
- Short-form vertical video (TikTok / Reels / Shorts) — primary use case.
- Image posts and multi-image carousels.
- Text posts (X, Threads, Bluesky, LinkedIn).
- Brand-templated clips produced in the Content Studio.

## Strengths
- Cheap and flat-priced — no per-channel fees; far below $75–$200/mo enterprise tools.
- Extremely simple, minimal learning curve; "2 minutes to post everywhere."
- Covers emerging platforms (Threads, Bluesky) that competitors often gate.
- Multiple accounts per platform and unlimited posts on paid tiers.
- Indie/founder-led: direct support, no cancellation lock-in.

## Weaknesses & gaps
- Analytics are shallow and still beta — no competitor tracking, custom reports, or deep insights.
- No AI caption/hashtag writing assistance.
- No white-label / agency features; small team can mean slower support.
- Restrictive free tier (5 posts) limits real evaluation without the trial.
- Content Studio is template-based, not a true AI video/UGC generator — the actual creative work still falls on the user.
- Less established brand than Buffer/Hootsuite/Later.

## Relevant to OpenShorts (steal / avoid)
- **Steal:** The "produce → queue in one continuous flow" Content Studio framing and a clean, low-friction library/composer — connect-once, upload-once, minimal-clicks UX. The radical simplicity and one-glance dashboard are a good north star for OpenShorts' library/results views. The flat, transparent, cheap pricing story (and indie "I built this to fix my own pain" positioning) is also worth noting.
- **Avoid / their gap we exploit:** Scheduling/cross-posting is **out of scope for OpenShorts V1** — we don't compete on distribution. Their real gap is that the *creative* output is just templates, not AI-generated UGC; OpenShorts' wedge is generating the actual high-quality vertical/UGC ad content, where Post-bridge merely moves it. We can be the upstream "make the content" layer that a tool like Post-bridge then distributes.
